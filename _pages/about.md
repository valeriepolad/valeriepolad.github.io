Hi! I'm a Ph.D. candidate in quantitative psychology at UNC Chapel Hill, where I work with Dan Bauer in the L. L. Thurstone Psychometric Laboratory. My research focuses on methods for detecting individual change over time. My dissertation compares the reliable change index with empirical Bayes estimates from hierarchical linear models. I'm also a data analyst at UNC's Winston National Center on Technology Use, Brain and Psychological Development, where I apply psychometric, longitudinal, and multilevel models to studies of adolescents' technology and AI use.

I teach statistics, too. I've been instructor of record for undergraduate statistics, led recitations for UNC's graduate ANOVA and regression sequence, and supported multilevel modeling courses at the ICPSR Summer Program. In all of these, I write annotated R code and tutorials so students can see why each step works, not just what to type.

I received a B.A. in Psychology from UCLA in 2020, with a minor in Cognitive Science and a specialization in computing, and an M.A. in Quantitative Psychology from UNC in 2025.
